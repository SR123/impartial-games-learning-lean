<!-- explain: c-takeaways -->
Three practical messages: single-frame, fixed-scale, bounded-depth inference is brittle on parity in the worst case;
the three bypasses supply different resources (a transition signal, complementary rollout channels, a growing
processing horizon); and even when a solution is expressible, discovering it from sparse win/loss rewards can be
hard, which is why the experiments use dense supervision.

<!-- formal: c-takeaways -->
A summary list. The first two items are covered by {{claim:thm:singleframe-impossible.b}} and
{{claim:prop:bypass-interfaces}}; the third is an observation about training, not a formal claim.

<!-- explain: c-noisy-teacher -->
The noisy teacher plays the optimal move (to nim-sum zero) with probability $1-\varepsilon$ and otherwise a uniformly
random legal move. Random moves broaden the positions seen and, for two-frame agents, create varied transition pairs
from which the difference signal can be learned.

Note: the dataset actually reported in [[sec:supervised-restoration]] is generated by a specific
teacher–random–teacher scheme (optimal move, then one uniformly random move, then the optimal labels), not by sampling
games from $\pi_T^\varepsilon$ with a stated $\varepsilon$.

<!-- formal: c-noisy-teacher -->
A definition used to describe data generation. It is not formalised and no theorem depends on it.

<!-- explain: c-majority-objection -->
A natural objection: real networks compute majority-like functions, so maybe $\mathrm{TC}^0$ (threshold circuits) is
the right model, not AC⁰. Indeed, with scale-adaptive thresholds or fan-in-dependent rescaling, constant-depth
threshold models *can* compute parity.

<!-- formal: c-majority-objection -->
A classical fact about threshold circuits, cited. The paper's model deliberately excludes such thresholds; the
checked boundary example is {{claim:rem:ac0-scope.model}}.

<!-- explain: c-majority-regime -->
The paper's answer: it studies a restricted regime (fixed total depth, nonnegative weights on Boolean literals,
thresholds bounded in units of the smallest weight). There each gate has a constant-size witness and collapses to a
constant-width DNF, so the whole model is in AC⁰ ([[thm:sim-ac0]]).

AC⁰ can still do a lot: it can approximately count (Ajtai). But AC⁰ functions have exponentially small correlation
with parity (Furst–Saxe–Sipser, Håstad, Smolensky, Razborov). Whether parity is *learnable* depends on the architecture
and training setting; the literature has both negative and positive results.

<!-- formal: c-majority-regime -->
The collapse is {{claim:thm:sim-ac0}} (checked). Approximate counting, the correlation bounds and the learning
literature are cited classical results; the correlation bound enters Lean only as the named input `HastadNegl`
({{claim:rem:local-vs-global-parity.global}}).

<!-- explain: c-fourier-basis -->
**Characters.** For a set $S$ of input coordinates, $\chi_S(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}x_i}$ is $+1$ or $-1$ according to the
parity of the inputs in $S$.

**Basis.** The $2^n$ characters are orthonormal under the uniform distribution, and every real function $f$ on
$\{0,1\}^n$ is a unique combination $f=\sum_S\hat f(S)\chi_S$ with coefficients $\hat f(S)=\mathbb E[f\chi_S]$.

**Example.** On two bits, AND (as a 0/1 function) is $\tfrac14-\tfrac14\chi_{\{1\}}-\tfrac14\chi_{\{2\}}+\tfrac14
\chi_{\{1,2\}}$.

The size $|S|$ is the *degree* or frequency of the coefficient. Parity of all inputs is the single highest-degree
character.

<!-- formal: c-fourier-basis -->
Checked over Mathlib's real numbers, for functions on Mathlib's finite cube `Fin n → Bool`:

- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.chiF_formula}}: $\chi_S(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}x_i}$ ({{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.chiF}});
- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.orthonormal_fin}}: orthonormality;
- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.inversion_fin}}: every $F$ equals the sum of its Fourier series
  (coefficients {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.fhat}} with the uniform normalisation);
- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.uniqueness_fin}}: the expansion is unique;
- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.parseval_fin}}: Parseval's identity.

All statements include $n=0$. The same results are proved for any linearly ordered field in the list form used by the
other modules (`Fourier`).

<!-- explain: def:fourier-tail -->
The tail above degree $k$ is the total squared Fourier weight on sets larger than $k$. A function is
$(k,\varepsilon)$-band-limited if that weight is at most $\varepsilon$: almost all of its "energy" is at low
frequency. For a $\pm1$-valued function the total weight is $1$, so the tail is the fraction of energy above degree
$k$.

<!-- formal: def:fourier-tail -->
{{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.tail}} is $\mathrm{Tail}_{>k}(f)=\sum_{|S|>k}\hat f(S)^2$ and
{{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.BandLimited}} is "tail at most $\varepsilon$". {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.tailF_eq}} connects
the Mathlib-cube version {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.tailF}} to the list-cube definition used elsewhere.

<!-- explain: eq:tail-correlation -->
**Parity is a pure character.** The parity of the inputs in a nonempty set $S$ is exactly $\chi_S$, so its Fourier
expansion has one coefficient, $1$ at $S$. Its frequency is $|S|$.

**The inequality.** The correlation of $f$ with $\chi_S$ *is* the coefficient $\hat f(S)$. If $|S|>k$, its square is
one term of the tail above $k$, hence at most $\varepsilon$ for a band-limited $f$. So: little high-frequency mass
implies little correlation with high-degree parities.

This elementary step is fully proved; what is external is the claim that AC⁰ functions are band-limited.

<!-- formal: eq:tail-correlation -->
- {{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.coef_chi}}: the coefficient of $\chi_S$ is $1$ at $S$ and $0$ elsewhere;
  {{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.chi_exactDeg}}: its exact degree is $|S|$.
- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.bandLimited_corr_real}}: over the reals, if $f$ is $(k,\varepsilon)$-band-limited and
  $|S|>k$, then $\langle f,\chi_S\rangle^2\le\varepsilon$.

<!-- explain: c-lmn -->
A classical theorem (Linial, Mansour and Nisan; sharpened by Tal): functions computed by constant-depth,
polynomial-size circuits have almost all of their Fourier mass at low degree. Consequently their correlation with full
parity is negligible.

**Caveats in the paper.** Applying this to parities of other subsets requires relating the subset size to the circuit
depth and size, and the uniform-distribution statement does not automatically transfer to a nonuniform training
distribution.

<!-- formal: c-lmn -->
Cited, not proved. In Lean the corresponding hypothesis is the named {{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.TailPremise}}, used only in
a conditional application. Matching it to a precise published statement is an open item
({{claim:c-tailpremise-open}}). The main single-channel bound does not use it.

<!-- explain: c-nim-bit-character -->
**Nim-sum bits are high-degree.** Bit $j$ of the nim-sum is the parity of the $j$-th bits of all $N$ heaps: a
character of degree $N$. As $N$ grows, it is exactly the kind of function AC⁰ correlates with negligibly.

**Delta bits with a known heap are low-degree.** If we know that heap $i$ changed, bit $j$ of the difference is
$x_{i,j}\oplus x'_{i,j}$: a character of degree $2$, trivially local.

<!-- formal: c-nim-bit-character -->
- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.nimBit_spectrum_real}}: the sign of nim-sum bit $j$ (on the $BN$-bit board encoding) has
  coefficient $1$ at one mask with exactly $N$ ones and $0$ elsewhere; its exact degree is $N$; its tail is $1$ below
  $N$ and $0$ from $N$ on. {{lean:NimAudit.Misc.nimsum_bit_character}} is the Boolean form.
- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.delta_spectrum_real}}: on the $2B$ bits of the changed heap, the sign of delta bit $j$ is
  the character of a two-element mask.
- {{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.nimBit_corr_of_tail}}: *if* the named {{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.TailPremise}} holds at some
  $k<N$, every bounded formula reading the board has squared correlation at most $\eta$ with nim-sum bit $j$. This is a
  conditional application; the premise is not instantiated.

<!-- explain: eq:delta-extension -->
**The problem.** When the changed heap is *not* known, the delta bit is only specified on pairs of boards that differ
in at most one heap. To talk about its Fourier expansion we need a function on *all* pairs.

**The extension used.** $d_j(P,P')=\bigvee_i(x_{i,j}\oplus x'_{i,j})$: "some heap differs in bit $j$". On pairs differing
in at most one heap this is exactly the delta bit, and it is computed by a shallow circuit.

**Its spectrum.** For $N\ge2$, the sign of $d_j$ is *not* a single character, not even up to sign; for $N=1$ it is
exactly a degree-two character. So "locally computable" does not mean "degree two" once the index is unknown.

**Caveat.** The promised domain contains every pair with at most one changed heap, including increases and unchanged
pairs, not only legal decreasing moves. The statement is about this particular extension, not about every possible
extension.

<!-- formal: eq:delta-extension -->
The extension is the circuit {{lean:NimAudit.LocalRestore.dC}} and its sign encoding {{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.dSign}} on
the full $2BN$-bit cube.

- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.dSign_enc_real}}: on every pair of $B$-bit boards of equal length differing in at most one
  heap, the sign of $d_j$ equals the sign of bit $j$ of $\operatorname{nim}(Q)\oplus\operatorname{nim}(Q')$.
- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.dC_not_character_real}} and {{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.dC_not_character}}: for $N\ge2$ and
  $j<B$ there is no mask $S$ and sign $c=\pm1$ with $\operatorname{sign}(d_j)=c\,\chi_S$ on the full cube.
- {{lean:NimAudit.RealFourier.dC_one_exactDeg_real}}: for $N=1$ it has exact degree $2$.
- {{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.gMask_delta}}: the global parity character of degree $2N$ (bit $j$ of every heap in both
  frames) agrees with the delta bit on every pair of boards; {{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.char_unique}}: it is the only
  character that agrees with the delta bit on all single-change pairs; {{lean:NimAudit.Fourier.no_low_degree_extension}}:
  hence no character of degree less than $2N$ does. These concern *all* single-change pairs (including increases and
  unchanged boards), not legal decreases only.

<!-- explain: c-spectral-bias -->
Gradient-trained networks are often observed to fit low-frequency components first ("spectral bias", "frequency
principle"). That suggests a possible analogy with parity learning. The paper is explicit that these continuous-domain
observations do not prove how SGD learns Boolean parity or restoration under its data distribution; it is a hypothesis
for separate measurement.

<!-- formal: c-spectral-bias -->
An informal hypothesis citing empirical literature; nothing is formalised or claimed.

<!-- explain: c-local-checks -->
The representational point in one sentence: the global invariant $\operatorname{nim}(P)$ is hard to compute from one
frame, while the transition quantity $\Delta(P,P')$ is local from two. The supervised experiment isolates exactly this
contrast by training one-frame and two-frame models on the same targets.
