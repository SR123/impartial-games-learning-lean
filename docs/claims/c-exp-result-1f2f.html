Section 7.2.5, “One-frame does not learn restoration; two-frame eventually does”1F stays near 0.075–0.081; 2F rises to 0.99998 test accuracy by epoch 178 (one saved run)

1 Paper text

One-frame does not learn restoration; two-frame eventually does. Figure 5 shows validation restoration accuracy as a function of epoch. The 1F model has low test restoration accuracy throughout training, approximately 0.075–0.081 across saved checkpoints. We do not identify this range with a measured random-legal-move baseline, which is not supplied in the reported evaluation. In contrast, the 2F model learns the restoration mapping and reaches essentially perfect accuracy with enough training. On the test set (Figure 6), the 2F checkpoints achieve 0.209 at epoch 1, 0.538 at epoch 23, 0.876 at epoch 45, 0.989 at epoch 67, and 0.99998 by epoch 178 (remaining ≥0.99993\ge 0.99993≥0.99993 at epoch 200).

2 Explain this

The result. In the saved run (N=20N=20N=20 heaps, k=4k=4k=4 bits, 10610^6106 examples), the 1F model stays at low test restoration accuracy throughout (about 0.075–0.081 across checkpoints). The 2F model learns: 0.209 at epoch 1, 0.538 at epoch 23, 0.876 at epoch 45, 0.989 at epoch 67 and 0.99998 by epoch 178 (still at least 0.99993 at epoch 200).

What it shows. Under this supervised protocol, transition information makes restoration much easier to learn.

What it does not show. That 1F is at a random-move baseline (no such baseline was measured), worst-case mastery, self-play learning, robustness across seeds, or anything about the asymptotic lower bound.
