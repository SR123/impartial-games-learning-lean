Remark 2.3 (local XOR)XOR of a constant number of bits or B-bit blocks is computable in AC0

1 Paper text

Remark 2.3 (Local vs. global parity) When we say “parity is hard” we mean global parity across the full Boolean input of length n=NBn=NBn=NB. In contrast, parity on O(1)O(1)O(1) bits is trivial in AC0\AC^0AC0 (hardwire the truth table), and more generally XOR of a constant number of BBB-bit blocks is AC0\AC^0AC0-computable. This local/global separation underlies both the two-frame bypass via the nimber difference Δ(P,P′)\Delta(P,P')Δ(P,P′) and the recurrent bypass, which composes local XOR updates over a growing number of steps.

2 Explain this

"Parity is hard" always means global parity of all n=NBn=NBn=NB input bits. Parity of a constant number of bits is easy: a fixed truth table, or a short chain of two-input XORs. Bitwise XOR of a constant number ttt of BBB-bit blocks is equally easy, since each output bit is the XOR of ttt bits.

This is what both bypasses exploit: the two-frame difference XORs two heaps, and the recurrent update XORs the accumulator with one row.

3 How this is formalised

Windows.xorChain_res: XOR of ttt formulas of depth at most ddd and size at most sss has depth at most d+3td+3td+3t and size at most 6t(s+1)6^t(s+1)6t(s+1). Windows.blockXor_res: bitwise XOR of ttt blocks has depth at most 3t3t3t and size at most 2⋅6t2\cdot6^t2⋅6t. For fixed ttt these are constants.
