Section C, “Where the layer counts come from”Origin and limitations of the 20-to-42 layer bound

Informal interpretation Motivation, interpretation or scope; not a formal claim.

1 Paper text

Where the layer counts come from. For the two-frame selector, the compositional Boolean-depth bound is 4+4+9+3=204+4+9+3=204+4+9+3=20: at most four levels extract the frame difference, four handle candidate tests and replacement bits, nine are allowed by the general selection construction (including fallback and output handling), and three encode the selected heap’s binary index. These are bounds on composed subcircuits, not necessarily the exact number of levels on a shortest implementation. The compiler then gives at most 2⋅20+2=422\cdot20+2=422⋅20+2=42 threshold layers. Similarly, Boolean depths 191919, 171717, 888 and 343434 give the other entries in the table.

2 Explain this

The number 42 comes from conservative accounting, not a training experiment or a lower bound. Difference extraction adds at most 4 Boolean levels; the candidate construction has bound 4; the generic selector allows 9 more; binary index encoding allows 3 more. The total bound 20 is converted to 2⋅20+2=422\cdot20+2=422⋅20+2=42 threshold layers. The generic compiler pads dependencies and reserves the two-layer cost of AND at every Boolean level. A specialised network may be smaller, but a sharper claim requires a new construction and proof.

The two-frame selector’s depth bound is a sum of bounds for composed subcircuits:

4493
  • 4 · frame difference
  • 4 · candidate tests and replacement bits
  • 9 · selection, fallback and output
  • 3 · binary index

Boolean depth ≤ 20 → the compiler’s rule 2d + 2 → at most 42 threshold layers.

The width counts every node of every output formula, duplicates included, so it is far from economical. These numbers describe the uniform construction, not an optimised network and not a trained one.

How this relates to the Lean proof. The grid is the output of ThresholdNet.compileF as Lean evaluates it on these formulas; the browser only evaluates the exported gates. ThresholdNet.compileF_spec proves, for any list of formulas of depth at most ddd, that the network has at most 2d+22d+22d+2 layers and computes every formula exactly. The selector bounds in the table of Appendix C come from this rule: Family.rest_ac0 (Boolean depth at most 20) and Family.rest_net (at most 42 layers with weights and thresholds 0 or 1).

What this does not show. The 42-layer figure is an upper bound for one explicit, deliberately uniform construction. It is not a lower bound, not a layer count measured in an experiment, and not a statement about which networks training finds. A more economical network may use fewer layers: for the small boards in the table, the selector formulas Lean builds have depth 15, so even the uniform compiler produces 32 layers, below the bound of 42. Proving a sharper general bound would need a separate construction and proof.

3 How this is formalised

The compositional bounds are LocalRestore.restC_bound (depth 4+9+44+9+44+9+4), PriorityEncoder.restBin_bound (depth 4+9+4+34+9+4+34+9+4+3), and ThresholdInst.restNet_res (at most 2⋅20+22\cdot20+22⋅20+2 layers). The general conversion is ThresholdNet.compileF_spec. These existing theorems bound this construction; they do not prove depth optimality or learnability.
