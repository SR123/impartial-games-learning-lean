Definition 4.1Finite terminating impartial games under normal play

External cited result A published result used as background or input; not proved in Lean here.

1 Paper text

Definition 4.1 (Impartial game) A finite terminating impartial game is a pair (P,M)(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{M})(P,M) where P\mathcal{P}P is a nonempty set of positions and M(p)⊆P\mathcal{M}(p)\subseteq \mathcal{P}M(p)⊆P is the (finite) set of legal moves from ppp, with the property that the move relation does not depend on which player is to play (symmetry). We assume the move relation is acyclic and play terminates. Under normal play, the player who makes the last move wins.

2 Explain this

An impartial game is one in which both players have the same moves from every position (unlike chess, where each player moves their own pieces). "Finite terminating" means play always ends; under normal play the player who makes the last move wins.

Example: NIM is impartial, since any player may reduce any heap.

3 How this is formalised

General impartial games and Sprague–Grundy theory are classical background (Berlekamp, Conway and Guy). The Lean development formalises NIM itself, not general impartial games; its normal-play semantics are in Definition 4.2 and Theorem 4.4.
