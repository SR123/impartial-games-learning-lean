Appendix G. Additional proofs for the rollout analysis

Generated from paper_jmlr.tex, lines 1624–1624.

2 Explain this

The full proof of Proposition 5.9: count the rollout moves of πj\pi_jπj​ in two phases (moves of size 2j2^j2j, then moves of size 111); read off the parity of each phase from the heaps' bits; recover all bits of σ\sigmaσ from the BBB outcomes; choose the first heap with hi⊕σ<hih_i\oplus\sigma<h_ihi​⊕σ<hi​ (one exists: a heap with the top bit of σ\sigmaσ); show that this selector wins against any legal opponent (after the selected move every opponent move makes the nim-sum nonzero, stones always decrease, and a nonterminal zero-nim-sum position cannot move straight to the end); and bound the total simulated moves by BN(2B−1)BN(2^B-1)BN(2B−1).

3 How this is formalised

Each step is checked: Rollout.run_length (move count under any tie-breaking), Rollout.run_parity (its parity is the channel bit), Game.reconstruct_eq (reconstruction), Rollout.exists_decreasing_heap (a cancelling heap exists), Game.agent_wins (the selector wins against every legal opponent) and Game.totalRolloutMoves_le (the step bound).

inductive Game.Wins

Module NimGame (lean/phase2/NimGame.lean, lines 125–132) · file SHA-256 23df6bb87ecfd344…

`Wins sel p`: the player to move at `p`, always playing the successor chosen by `sel`, wins against EVERY legal opponent. The selected move is legal, and after every legal opponent reply the player is again in a `Wins` position. Because the predicate is inductive, every such play is finite and ends with the opponent facing a terminal board (normal play: the opponent loses).
