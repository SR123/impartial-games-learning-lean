Theorem 5.2 (barrier)No polynomial-size, constant-depth fixed-scale network family strongly masters NIM

Checked conditional theorem using named classical input Lean checks the implication from a named, unproved classical hypothesis.

1 Paper text

2 Explain this

The barrier. Put the reduction together with the simulation theorem: a polynomial-size, constant-depth fixed-scale network that strongly masters NIM would give AC0 circuits computing parity of mmm bits for every mmm. Parity is not in AC0, so no such network family exists.

Why it matters. This is the obstruction behind the whole paper: a single board, processed by a shallow fixed-scale network in one pass, does not give access to the global invariant.

Caveats. It is a worst-case statement within the restricted model (some boards defeat the network, and the encoded family is enough). It does not say that every learned network fails on typical positions, and it says nothing about networks outside the model.

3 How this is formalised

Named classical input. Barrier.ParityNotAC0 is the classical worst-case statement in family form; it is a theorem parameter, never an axiom. ClassicalBoundary.parityNotAC0_of_hastad derives it from ClassicalBoundary.HastadNegl, the negligible-correlation form of Håstad (2014).

Checked barrier.

  • Barrier.barrier_fam: under ParityNotAC0, no AC0 formula family of binary selectors plays a winning move on every encoded board.
  • Barrier.barrier_net: the same for fixed-scale network families (fixed WWW, at most LLL layers, polynomial widths and fan-ins) whose output wires are decoded as a binary move. The proof compiles the networks to formulas (Theorem 2.4) and applies the extraction.

Relationship to the paper. The paper says "must compute the parity"; Lean proves the extraction exactly. The paper says "no network can strongly master NIM"; Lean proves that no family wins on every board of the encoded subfamily, which is stronger. Parity hardness itself is the external input.

Prose-to-formal notes

Lean proves: if ParityNotAC0 holds, no such network family plays a winning move on every encoded board; and HastadNegl implies ParityNotAC0. Parity hardness itself is the external classical input.
