Section 6.4, “Local vs. global parity in NIM”A nim-sum bit is a degree-N character; for a known changed heap a delta bit is a two-coordinate character

Checked theorem Lean checks a formal statement corresponding to this claim.

1 Paper text

Local vs. global parity in NIM. In our encoding, the jjjth bit of the nim-sum is the parity of the jjjth bits across all NNN heaps, hence a degree-NNN character. Conditional on a specified changed heap iii, the sign encoding of the jjjth delta bit is the two-coordinate character (−1)xi,j+xi,j′(-1)^{x_{i,j}+x'_{i,j}}(−1)xi,j​+xi,j′​. When the changed index is unknown, the delta bit is specified on pairs of boards differing in at most one heap. A shallow extension to all pairs is dj(P,P′)=⋁i=1N(xi,j⊕xi,j′).d_j(P,P')=\bigvee_{i=1}^{N}(x_{i,j}\oplus x'_{i,j}).dj​(P,P′)=i=1⋁N​(xi,j​⊕xi,j′​).(6.2) On the promised domain this is the required delta bit. For N≥2N\ge2N≥2, its sign encoding (−1)dj(-1)^{d_j}(−1)dj​ is not a single character, even up to sign; for N=1N=1N=1 it is exactly a degree-two character. Thus local computability does not mean that the full unknown-index map has degree two. It follows from the explicit AC0\AC^0AC0 construction in Lemma 5.4.

2 Explain this

Nim-sum bits are high-degree. Bit jjj of the nim-sum is the parity of the jjj-th bits of all NNN heaps: a character of degree NNN. As NNN grows, it is exactly the kind of function AC0 correlates with negligibly.

Delta bits with a known heap are low-degree. If we know that heap iii changed, bit jjj of the difference is xi,j⊕xi,j′x_{i,j}\oplus x'_{i,j}xi,j​⊕xi,j′​: a character of degree 222, trivially local.

3 How this is formalised

  • RealFourier.nimBit_spectrum_real: the sign of nim-sum bit jjj (on the BNBNBN-bit board encoding) has coefficient 111 at one mask with exactly NNN ones and 000 elsewhere; its exact degree is NNN; its tail is 111 below NNN and 000 from NNN on. Misc.nimsum_bit_character is the Boolean form.
  • RealFourier.delta_spectrum_real: on the 2B2B2B bits of the changed heap, the sign of delta bit jjj is the character of a two-element mask.
  • Fourier.nimBit_corr_of_tail: if the named Fourier.TailPremise holds at some k<Nk<Nk<N, every bounded formula reading the board has squared correlation at most η\etaη with nim-sum bit jjj. This is a conditional application; the premise is not instantiated.
