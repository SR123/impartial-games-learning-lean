Example 1Pairing strategy: a constant-depth mirror selector wins as second player on paired boards

Checked theorem Lean checks a formal statement corresponding to this claim.

1 Paper text

Example 1 (Pairing strategy) Consider positions (a,a,b,b,c,c,… )(a,a,b,b,c,c,\dots)(a,a,b,b,c,c,…) consisting of pairs of equal heaps. The second player can mirror the first player’s move on the matching heap, maintaining the paired structure until termination, hence winning. The checked binary selector finds the first unequal pair and reduces its larger heap to match its partner. It has constant depth and polynomial size, and wins against every legal opponent from the paired family when playing second. For the first-player role every paired initial board is losing, so weak mastery there would be vacuous.

2 Explain this

On boards made of equal pairs, (a,a,b,b,c,c,… )(a,a,b,b,c,c,\dots)(a,a,b,b,c,c,…), the second player can copy every move on the partner heap. The pairing is restored after each reply, so the second player always has a move and makes the last one.

The checked selector finds the first unequal pair and reduces its larger heap to match its partner; it has constant depth and polynomial size and uses no nim-sum. For the first-player role every paired board is losing, so weak mastery there would be vacuous.

3 How this is formalised

  • Misc.pairing_wins: from every paired board (Misc.Paired) of width BBB, the reply rule computed by the local binary mirror circuit wins as second player against every legal opponent.
  • Misc.pairR_correct: after a legal move from a paired board, the mirror circuit outputs the copying reply.
  • Misc.pairBin_bound: constant depth (at most 10) and polynomial size.
  • Mastery.pairing_net_weak: the compiled network's strategy is weakly masterful for the second player on the paired families, in the general history model.
  • Mastery.pairing_first_vacuous: for the first player the paired family is losing, so weak mastery there is vacuous.
