2 Explain this

Statement. For 0<a≤1/20<a\le1/20<a≤1/2:   3/(32a2)≤m∗(a)≤⌈4/a2⌉≤17/(4a2)\;3/(32a^2)\le m_*(a)\le\lceil4/a^2\rceil\le17/(4a^2)3/(32a2)≤m∗​(a)≤⌈4/a2⌉≤17/(4a2), and majority vote with ties declared 000 achieves the middle bound.

Upper bound idea. The number of correct observations has mean m(1+a)/2m(1+a)/2m(1+a)/2 and variance at most m/4m/4m/4; Chebyshev's inequality gives majority error at most 1/(ma2)1/(ma^2)1/(ma2), which is at most 1/41/41/4 once m≥4/a2m\ge4/a^2m≥4/a2.

Lower bound idea. If ma2<3/32ma^2<3/32ma2<3/32, the two possible observation laws are so close (chi-square divergence below 111, hence total variation below 1/21/21/2) that no rule, randomised or not, can have both errors at most 1/41/41/4.

Example. a=0.364a=0.364a=0.364 (from ε=0.01\varepsilon=0.01ε=0.01, L=50L=50L=50): ⌈4/a2⌉=31\lceil4/a^2\rceil=31⌈4/a2⌉=31 observations suffice.
