Section 6.1, listPractical takeaways: single-frame brittleness, three bypasses, optimisation still hard

Informal interpretation Motivation, interpretation or scope; not a formal claim.

1 Paper text

  • Single-frame is brittle to parity. In the worst case, fixed-scale quantised bounded-depth inference cannot reliably represent the nim-sum or a winning move selector (Section 5.2).

  • Three bypasses with different resources. A two-frame input exposes the transition signal Δ(P,P′)\Delta(P,P')Δ(P,P′) and supports restoration (Section 5.3.1); multiple rollout heads recover the missing nim-sum bits through depth amplification (Section 5.4); persistent recurrence accumulates the invariant over sequential row updates and retains summaries across positions (Section 5.5). The last route trades fixed total latency for a growing processing horizon.

  • Optimisation remains nontrivial. Even when a solution is expressible in AC0\AC^0AC0, discovering it from sparse win/loss rewards can be difficult; our experiments therefore isolate a dense restoration supervision signal and separately report rollout-based classification.

2 Explain this

Three practical messages: single-frame, fixed-scale, bounded-depth inference is brittle on parity in the worst case; the three bypasses supply different resources (a transition signal, complementary rollout channels, a growing processing horizon); and even when a solution is expressible, discovering it from sparse win/loss rewards can be hard, which is why the experiments use dense supervision.

A summary list. The first two items are covered by Theorem 5.2 (barrier) and Proposition 5.14; the third is an observation about training, not a formal claim.
