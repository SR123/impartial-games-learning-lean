Section 7.2.5, paragraph 3Staged acquisition at the output level; no internal mechanism and no 8/15 explanation are established

Informal interpretation Motivation, interpretation or scope; not a formal claim.

1 Paper text

Thus the later improvement is accounted for at the output level by learning to select moves with the correct lowest bit while retaining the already accurate upper bits. This supports staged acquisition of the restoration behaviour. It does not identify an internal representation or explain why optimisation follows this order. Nor does the plateau’s proximity to 8/158/158/15 establish a random-bit guessing mechanism: that baseline assumes a particular distribution and prediction rule. Internal probes, interventions and independent training runs are needed to explain the transition and test its reproducibility.

2 Explain this

At the level of outputs, the later improvement consists of getting the lowest bit of the change right while keeping the upper bits. That supports "staged acquisition". The paper is careful about what it does not conclude:

  • it does not identify an internal representation or explain why optimisation follows this order;
  • the plateau's closeness to 8/15≈0.5338/15\approx0.5338/15≈0.533 does not establish a random-bit guessing mechanism (that number assumes a particular distribution and prediction rule);
  • probes, interventions and independent runs would be needed.

3 How this is formalised

Interpretation of empirical data. The arithmetic fact 8/158/158/15 (the fraction of nonzero 4-bit nim-sums with lowest bit 111) is checked (Section 7.3, paragraph 3 (arithmetic)); whether it has anything to do with the plateau is not established.
