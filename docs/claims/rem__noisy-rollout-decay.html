Appendix F states and proves explicit bounds over real parameters. Repeating one corrupted observation with the same randomness supplies no additional information. 2 Explain this

The model. Suppose the ideal outcome bit YYY reaches us flipped by LLL independent coin flips, each with probability ε<1/2\varepsilon<1/2ε<1/2. The observed bit agrees with YYY with probability (1+a)/2(1+a)/2(1+a)/2 where a=(1−2ε)La=(1-2\varepsilon)^La=(1−2ε)L: each flip multiplies the bias by 1−2ε1-2\varepsilon1−2ε.

Example. ε=0.01\varepsilon=0.01ε=0.01, L=50L=50L=50: a≈0.364a\approx0.364a≈0.364 and agreement ≈0.682\approx0.682≈0.682, far from a fair coin.

Repetitions. With fresh independent flips each time (and 0≤ε<1/20\le\varepsilon<1/20≤ε<1/2), about 1/a21/a^21/a2 observations are needed to decide YYY with error at most 1/41/41/4 for each value of YYY. When 0<a≤1/20<a\le1/20<a≤1/2, every (possibly randomised) decision rule needs at least 3/(32a2)3/(32a^2)3/(32a2) observations, while majority vote achieves the target with at most 17/(4a2)17/(4a^2)17/(4a2). These bounds therefore enclose the minimum sufficient sample count. In the example, 31 fresh repetitions with majority vote (ties declared 000) suffice.

Caveats.

  • Repeating the same corrupted observation (same randomness) gives no new information.
  • Correlated flips can cancel in pairs, so the decay is not automatic.
  • This models noise on the outcome channel, not erroneous game moves: a wrong move need not flip the outcome and can change everything that follows. Exact structured rollouts have no noise at all.
