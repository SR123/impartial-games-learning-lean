Proposition 5.14Each bypass: shallow {0,1}-weight components and a complete strategy winning against every legal opponent

Checked theorem Lean checks a formal statement corresponding to this claim.

1 Paper text

Proposition 5.14 (Shallow components and complete strategies) For N≥1N\ge1N≥1 heaps of B≥1B\ge1B≥1 bits, the following constructions use polynomial-size, constant-depth Boolean components, realisable by literal-threshold networks with weights and thresholds in {0,1}\{0,1\}{0,1}.

  1. (i) The two-frame selector maps a legal transition Q→Q′Q\to Q'Q→Q′ from nim⁡(Q)=0\operatorname{nim}(Q)=0nim(Q)=0 to a legal binary-encoded response restoring zero. Retaining the response board as the next predecessor gives a winning strategy from this established invariant, with the opponent to move.

  2. (ii) Each structured rollout policy and the readout from the board and its BBB outcome bits are shallow. Executing the rollouts and then the readout gives a winning strategy from any nonzero nim-sum board, using at most BN(2B−1)BN(2^B-1)BN(2B−1) simulated game moves per decision.

  3. (iii) The recurrent state update is shallow. Iterating it over the replay protocol gives a winning strategy from any nonzero nim-sum board, with O(B+log⁡N)O(B+\log N)O(B+logN) memory and 2N+22N+22N+2 updates per decision.

Here “winning strategy” quantifies over every legal opponent response. Polynomial size and constant depth refer to the indicated component, with depth independent of NNN and BBB; they do not bound the number of sequential component evaluations.

2 Explain this

What it says. For each bypass, the shallow pieces are genuinely shallow (polynomial size, constant depth, realisable with weights and thresholds in {0,1}\{0,1\}{0,1}), and running them as prescribed gives a strategy that wins against every legal opponent:

  1. Two frames: the selector maps a legal transition from a zero-nim-sum board to a restoring reply; keeping the reply as the next predecessor wins from the established invariant.
  2. Rollouts: each policy step and the final readout are shallow; executing the rollouts, then the readout, wins from any nonzero board with at most BN(2B−1)BN(2^B-1)BN(2B−1) simulated moves per decision.
  3. Recurrence: the state update is shallow; iterating it over the replay protocol wins from any nonzero board with O(B+log⁡N)O(B+\log N)O(B+logN) memory and 2N+22N+22N+2 updates per decision.

The fine print. "Polynomial size and constant depth" refer to one component evaluation, with depth independent of NNN and BBB. They do not bound how many evaluations happen in sequence.

All three routes use shallow (AC0) components. The complete rollout and recurrent board-to-move computations are not AC0 families, given the named classical input that parity is not in AC0: otherwise they would give shallow parity circuits. Their sequential time grows with the board; that growing computation is the resource the single-frame model lacks. The two-frame route instead changes the input and relies on a promise.

How this relates to the Lean proof. Bypass.prop_bypass_interfaces packages the three constructions with their shallow components and complete winning strategies; Bypass.bypass_barriers records why the complete rollout and recurrent maps cannot themselves be AC0\mathrm{AC}^0AC0 families, assuming the named classical input that parity is not in AC0\mathrm{AC}^0AC0 (Barrier.ParityNotAC0).

What this does not show. Nothing here claims that self-play training discovers these constructions, and the restricted model is not a model of every neural network.

3 How this is formalised

Bypass.prop_bypass_interfaces states all three parts at once. For each bypass it gives an AC0 family certificate (Family.AC0Fam) and a {0,1}\{0,1\}{0,1}-weight network family certificate (Family.NetFam) for the shallow components, with explicit depth and layer bounds, together with the complete strategy:

  • (i) two frames: formulas of depth at most 20, networks of at most 42 layers, and WholeGame.twoFrame_wins in the stateful predicate WholeGame.TwoFrameWins;
  • (ii) rollouts: policy step depth at most 17 (36 layers), register step depth at most 28 (58 layers), readout depth at most 19 (40 layers), and RolloutExec.mhExec_wins with the move bound;
  • (iii) recurrence: the controller update and the replay strategy, WholeGame.replay_wins; the board-storage variant is BoardStore.bits_store_wins.

Semantics used. The winning guarantees for (i) and (iii) are proved in their own stateful game predicates (TwoFrameWins, WholeGame.ReplayWins, BitStoreWins). The two-frame strategy is also connected to the general weak-mastery history model; the recurrent strategies are not (see the open item below).

Prose-to-formal notes

The recurrent winning guarantees (part iii) are proved in their own stateful game semantics (ReplayWins, BitStoreWins), not in the general history model of Definition 5.1; see the open item on that bridge.
