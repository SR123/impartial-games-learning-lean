Equation (6.2)The total OR-of-XORs extension equals Δ's bit on single-change pairs; not a character for N≥2; degree two for N=1

Checked theorem Lean checks a formal statement corresponding to this claim.

1 Paper text

Local vs. global parity in NIM. In our encoding, the jjjth bit of the nim-sum is the parity of the jjjth bits across all NNN heaps, hence a degree-NNN character. Conditional on a specified changed heap iii, the sign encoding of the jjjth delta bit is the two-coordinate character (−1)xi,j+xi,j′(-1)^{x_{i,j}+x'_{i,j}}(−1)xi,j​+xi,j′​. When the changed index is unknown, the delta bit is specified on pairs of boards differing in at most one heap. A shallow extension to all pairs is dj(P,P′)=⋁i=1N(xi,j⊕xi,j′).d_j(P,P')=\bigvee_{i=1}^{N}(x_{i,j}\oplus x'_{i,j}).dj​(P,P′)=i=1⋁N​(xi,j​⊕xi,j′​).(6.2) On the promised domain this is the required delta bit. For N≥2N\ge2N≥2, its sign encoding (−1)dj(-1)^{d_j}(−1)dj​ is not a single character, even up to sign; for N=1N=1N=1 it is exactly a degree-two character. Thus local computability does not mean that the full unknown-index map has degree two. It follows from the explicit AC0\AC^0AC0 construction in Lemma 5.4.

2 Explain this

The problem. When the changed heap is not known, the delta bit is only specified on pairs of boards that differ in at most one heap. To talk about its Fourier expansion we need a function on all pairs.

The extension used. dj(P,P′)=⋁i(xi,j⊕xi,j′)d_j(P,P')=\bigvee_i(x_{i,j}\oplus x'_{i,j})dj​(P,P′)=⋁i​(xi,j​⊕xi,j′​): "some heap differs in bit jjj". On pairs differing in at most one heap this is exactly the delta bit, and it is computed by a shallow circuit.

Its spectrum. For N≥2N\ge2N≥2, the sign of djd_jdj​ is not a single character, not even up to sign; for N=1N=1N=1 it is exactly a degree-two character. So "locally computable" does not mean "degree two" once the index is unknown.

Caveat. The promised domain contains every pair with at most one changed heap, including increases and unchanged pairs, not only legal decreasing moves. The statement is about this particular extension, not about every possible extension.

3 How this is formalised

The extension is the circuit LocalRestore.dC and its sign encoding Fourier.dSign on the full 2BN2BN2BN-bit cube.

  • RealFourier.dSign_enc_real: on every pair of BBB-bit boards of equal length differing in at most one heap, the sign of djd_jdj​ equals the sign of bit jjj of nim⁡(Q)⊕nim⁡(Q′)\operatorname{nim}(Q)\oplus\operatorname{nim}(Q')nim(Q)⊕nim(Q′).
  • RealFourier.dC_not_character_real and Fourier.dC_not_character: for N≥2N\ge2N≥2 and j<Bj<Bj<B there is no mask SSS and sign c=±1c=\pm1c=±1 with sign⁡(dj)=c χS\operatorname{sign}(d_j)=c\,\chi_Ssign(dj​)=cχS​ on the full cube.
  • RealFourier.dC_one_exactDeg_real: for N=1N=1N=1 it has exact degree 222.
  • Fourier.gMask_delta: the global parity character of degree 2N2N2N (bit jjj of every heap in both frames) agrees with the delta bit on every pair of boards; Fourier.char_unique: it is the only character that agrees with the delta bit on all single-change pairs; Fourier.no_low_degree_extension: hence no character of degree less than 2N2N2N does. These concern all single-change pairs (including increases and unchanged boards), not legal decreases only.

Prose-to-formal notes

The claim concerns the one specified extension djd_jdj​, not every extension of the partial delta function. A separate checked theorem shows that the degree-2N2N2N global character is the unique character agreeing with the delta bit on all single-change pairs; it is not a statement about legal decreases only.
