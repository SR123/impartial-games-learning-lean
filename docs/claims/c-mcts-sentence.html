Section 5.3.1, paragraph 3The significance of two frames for learning is representational

Informal interpretation Motivation, interpretation or scope; not a formal claim.

1 Paper text

The significance for learnability is representational. A two-frame policy network can condition its response on (Q,Q′)(Q,Q')(Q,Q′) and hence on the locally computable δ\deltaδ, while a single-frame policy must reconstruct the relevant global information from Q′Q'Q′ alone. The experiments in Section 7 examine this contrast through supervised restoration learning and imitation-trained rollout policies. The reported networks are policy-only; these results do not establish that self-play discovers the constructions.

Generated from paper_jmlr.tex, lines 609–609.

2 Explain this

The advantage of two frames is about what a network can represent: a two-frame policy can condition on the locally computable difference, while a single-frame policy must reconstruct the global invariant from one board.

3 How this is formalised

An interpretive paragraph; the formal contrast is Proposition 5.5 versus Theorem 5.2 (barrier).

The reported Section 7 results concern supervised restoration and imitation-trained rollout policies, with no value head (Section 7.1). They do not establish self-play discovery. An auxiliary MCTS tournament exists in the restoration notebook, but it is separate from those reported results and is not self-play training. The manuscript's earlier reference to experimental policy/value networks was corrected in the focused revision.
