Section 5.4, “The initialisation boundary for restoration”Matching deltas preserves nim(Q); a completed matching-delta policy loses from the winning position (2)

Checked theorem Lean checks a formal statement corresponding to this claim.

1 Paper text

The initialisation boundary for restoration. Matching two successive differences yields nim⁡(R)=nim⁡(Q)\operatorname{nim}(R)=\operatorname{nim}(Q)nim(R)=nim(Q), whether or not that value is zero. Thus Proposition 5.5 does not supply a classifier for an arbitrary starting position. For example, complete a matching-delta policy by subtracting one from the first nonempty heap whenever no matching response exists or no previous frame is available. Starting from (2)(2)(2), it plays (2)→(1)→(0)(2)\to(1)\to(0)(2)→(1)→(0) and the first player loses, although (2)(2)(2) is winning. The fallback after the first move is needed because its delta is 2⊕1=32\oplus1=32⊕1=3, which cannot be realised from (1)(1)(1). This counterexample does not rule out every possible verifier construction; it shows that conditional restoration and an unspecified fallback do not establish one. We make no universal-verifier claim. Initial winning-move selection can instead use the multi-channel construction above or the growing recurrent scan in Section 5.5.

2 Explain this

Matching the last difference only says nim⁡(R)=nim⁡(Q)\operatorname{nim}(R)=\operatorname{nim}(Q)nim(R)=nim(Q). If QQQ did not have nim-sum zero, neither does RRR. So restoration cannot, by itself, tell whether an arbitrary starting position is winning.

Counterexample. Complete a matching-delta policy by "subtract one from the first nonempty heap" whenever no matching reply exists or no previous frame is available. From (2)(2)(2), a winning position, the first player plays (2)→(1)(2)\to(1)(2)→(1) (no previous frame). The opponent's reply from (1)(1)(1) is computed by the same policy: the difference to match is 2⊕1=32\oplus1=32⊕1=3, which cannot be realised from (1)(1)(1), so it falls back and plays (1)→(0)(1)\to(0)(1)→(0). The first player has lost from a winning position.

What it shows: conditional restoration plus an unspecified fallback is not a verifier. It does not rule out every possible verifier. Initial moves can come from the multi-channel construction or the recurrent scan instead.

3 How this is formalised

  • Misc.init_counterexample checks the counterexample step by step: (2)(2)(2) is winning; the completed policy Misc.mdPolicy plays (2)→(1)(2)\to(1)(2)→(1); no move from (1)(1)(1) cancels 2⊕12\oplus12⊕1; the fallback plays (1)→(0)(1)\to(0)(1)→(0); (1)(1)(1) is winning and (0)(0)(0) is losing.
  • Rollout.matching_deltas_do_not_force_zero: the universal statement "matching deltas forces nim-sum zero" is false without the zero promise; no_zero_without_zero_promise is the concrete instance 1⊕3=3⊕11\oplus3=3\oplus11⊕3=3⊕1 with 1≠01\ne01=0.
