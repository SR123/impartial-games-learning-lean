Appendix D, paragraph 1From (3), taking all stones weakly masters the position but is not strong mastery

Checked theorem Lean checks a formal statement corresponding to this claim.

1 Paper text

The distinction from strong mastery is substantive. From the single heap (3)(3)(3), a strategy that takes all three stones wins immediately, irrespective of what it would do elsewhere. It therefore weakly masters that initial position as first player. If it has no prescribed move at (1)(1)(1), however, it fails strong mastery: arbitrary legal play can reach (3)→(2)→(1)(3)\to(2)\to(1)(3)→(2)→(1) with the first player to move. That history is excluded by its own initial action. The general definition and this separation are checked in Lean.

2 Explain this

From the single heap (3)(3)(3), the strategy "take all three stones" wins at once, so it weakly masters (3)(3)(3) as first player, whatever it would do elsewhere. But it prescribes nothing at (1)(1)(1), and arbitrary legal play can reach (3)→(2)→(1)(3)\to(2)\to(1)(3)→(2)→(1) with the first player to move. Strong mastery requires a winning move there, so this strategy is not strongly masterful. That history is excluded from weak mastery because it contradicts the strategy's own first move.

3 How this is formalised

Mastery.sep3 is the strategy "at (3)(3)(3) play (0)(0)(0), otherwise nothing". Mastery.sep3_weak: it is weakly masterful for the family {(3)}\{(3)\}{(3)} and the first role. Mastery.sep3_not_strong: it is not strongly masterful (after the counterfactual 3→23\to23→2 and 2→12\to12→1 it has no move at the NNN-position (1)(1)(1)). Mastery.strong_weak: strong mastery always implies weak mastery.
