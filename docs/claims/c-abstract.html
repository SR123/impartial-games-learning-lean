1 Paper text

Abstract

Why can neural networks struggle to learn optimal play in a game with a simple, efficient solution algorithm? Motivated by empirical difficulties with parity in impartial games, we use NIM to study a representational bottleneck and three ways around it. We show that a restricted model of shallow, fixed-scale quantised networks lies in AC0\AC^0AC0, a circuit class unable to compute parity on arbitrarily large inputs. This prevents optimal single-position play across all board sizes within the model. The obstruction also guides architectural design: two-frame inputs allow restoration from a known zero-nim-sum position; complementary rollout policies reveal the missing invariant; and persistent recurrence accumulates it row by row. Each route retains shallow network components while supplying additional information or processing time. Supervised experiments illustrate the benefits of two-frame restoration and multiple rollout heads; recurrence is a theoretical construction. These results establish representational possibilities, not guarantees of successful training: AC0\AC^0AC0 does not characterise practical learnability. Core mathematical results and constructions have been checked in Lean, with classical complexity results as explicit external inputs. The interactive edition and complete Lean sources are available online (Riis, 2026a,b).

2 Explain this

The abstract compresses the whole paper into a few sentences. Each sentence corresponds to a precise statement inside the paper, and several of them carry conditions that the abstract can only hint at:

  • "lies in AC0" is Theorem 2.4, for an explicitly restricted model of fixed-scale threshold networks, not for neural networks in general;
  • "prevents optimal single-position play" is Theorem 5.2, which uses the classical fact that parity is not in AC0;
  • the three routes around the obstruction are Proposition 5.5 (two frames, given a zero-nim-sum predecessor), Proposition 5.9 (several rollout policies, executed in sequence) and Proposition 5.13 (a recurrent scan whose length grows with the board, with retained state, a reset, and a replay of the board or board storage to choose a move);
  • the experiments are single saved supervised and imitation runs (Section 7.2, Section 7.3).

The learnability qualification says what is not claimed: the constructions are representational possibilities, not guarantees that training will find them, and AC0 membership is not a test of learnability.
