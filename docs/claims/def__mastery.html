Definition 5.1Strong, weak and restoration mastery

Checked definition or construction Lean contains the definition or construction, with checked properties.

1 Paper text

Definition 5.1 (Strong, weak, and restoration mastery) An agent achieves strong mastery of NIM if it chooses a winning move at every NNN-position where it is to move, including positions reached by arbitrary legal play by both players. Reachability is relative to the specified initial positions and player role; unrestricted strong mastery allows every initial position.

It achieves weak mastery relative to an initial position p0p_0p0​ and a designated player role if, whenever that role has a winning strategy from p0p_0p0​, its own strategy wins against every opponent strategy. The guarantee concerns histories consistent with its own preceding actions, not every position reachable under arbitrary actions by both players. For asymptotic claims, the initial positions form a specified family. Strategies may depend on the complete history. Opponent moves are legal; a missing or illegal agent move counts as a loss. On an initially losing position the weak-mastery condition imposes no requirement.

It achieves restoration mastery if, whenever the opponent moves from a PPP-position QQQ with nim⁡(Q)=0\operatorname{nim}(Q)=0nim(Q)=0 to a position Q′Q'Q′, the agent responds with a move Q′→RQ'\to RQ′→R such that nim⁡(R)=0\operatorname{nim}(R)=0nim(R)=0.

2 Explain this

The paper uses three standards of "playing NIM well".

  • Strong mastery: at every winning position where it is to move, the agent chooses a winning move, including positions that only arise after mistakes by either player.
  • Weak mastery (relative to an initial position and a role): if that role can win from the start, the agent's own strategy wins against every opponent. Positions that the agent's own play never reaches do not matter.
  • Restoration mastery: whenever the opponent moves away from a zero-nim-sum position, the agent moves back to nim-sum zero.

Example of the difference. From the single heap (3)(3)(3), the strategy "take all three stones" wins at once, so it weakly masters (3)(3)(3) as first player. It says nothing about (1)(1)(1), which strong mastery would require after (3)→(2)→(1)(3)\to(2)\to(1)(3)→(2)→(1) (see Appendix D).

Caveats spelled out in the definition. Strategies may use the whole history. Opponent moves are legal; a missing or illegal agent move counts as a loss. If the designated role is losing from the initial position, weak mastery asks nothing at all: it is vacuous there.

3 How this is formalised

Histories and strategies. A history (Mastery.Hist) is the list of boards so far, most recent first; a strategy (Mastery.Strat) maps a history to a proposed next board or to "no action".

Consistency. Mastery.Cons defines the histories consistent with the agent's own play from an initial board P0P_0P0​ in role rrr: on the agent's turns only its own prescribed legal action occurs; on the opponent's turns every legal move may occur.

The notions.

  • Mastery.Guarantee: the strategy wins from P0P_0P0​ in role rrr against every legal opponent strategy (plays are finite because stones decrease).
  • Mastery.WeakMastery F r σF\ r\ \sigmaF r σ: from every initial board in the family FFF from which role rrr has some winning strategy (Mastery.RoleWinnable), σ\sigmaσ has the guarantee.
  • Mastery.StrongMastery F r σF\ r\ \sigmaF r σ: at every legal history from FFF (arbitrary actions by both players) where the agent is to move at a nonzero nim-sum, σ\sigmaσ moves to nim-sum zero.
  • Restoration: Game.restore_correct shows that the two-frame restoration selector has this property under its promise.

Vacuity, made explicit. Mastery.weakMastery_vacuous: on a family of boards from which the role is losing, every strategy, even one that never moves, is weakly masterful. Strong mastery implies weak mastery (Mastery.strong_weak), and the separation example is Appendix D, paragraph 1.

Assumptions and domain

A designated role (first or second player) and a family of initial boards; deterministic, history-dependent strategies; legal opponents; a missing or illegal agent move is a loss.

Prose-to-formal notes

Weak mastery quantifies over histories consistent with the agent's own earlier actions; strong mastery over all legal histories. On an initially losing board the weak-mastery condition is vacuous, and Lean states this explicitly.
