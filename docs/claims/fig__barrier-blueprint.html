2 Explain this

The figure is a map of the paper. Row (a) is the obstruction: a single network that sees one board cannot compute global parity under the fixed-scale model. Rows (b)–(d) are the three bypasses, each supplying something different: (b) the transition between two boards, given a zero-nim-sum predecessor; (c) the outcomes of complete rollouts under several policy heads; (d) a recurrent state carried over many row updates, so that total latency grows with the number of heaps.

Caveat: a check mark in the figure means "this computation becomes available", not "learning succeeds".

3 How this is formalised

A schematic, not a formal statement. Each row corresponds to checked results: (a) Theorem 5.2 (barrier); (b) Proposition 5.5 and Section 5.3.1, paragraph 2; (c) Proposition 5.9; (d) Proposition 5.13 and Section 5.5, “From a summary to a move”.
