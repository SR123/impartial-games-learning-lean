Section 10, paragraph 4Author review of the paper–Lean correspondence is distinct from manual inspection of every proof

Informal interpretation Motivation, interpretation or scope; not a formal claim.

1 Paper text

The author’s review of the formalisation focused on the interface between the paper and Lean: whether the formal definitions, hypotheses and conclusions express the intended mathematical claims. The author did not manually inspect every theorem or lemma declaration or every line of Lean source. Mechanical checking verifies the formal proofs relative to their definitions and stated hypotheses; it does not independently certify the correspondence with the prose, the suitability of the modelling assumptions, or the experimental results.

2 Explain this

There are two distinct checks: whether Lean accepts a proof of a formal statement, and whether that statement correctly expresses the intended paper claim. The author reports reviewing the second interface without manually reading every helper proof. The linked statements, definitions and source allow readers to examine both levels.

3 How this is formalised

The reported development contains 1,588 theorem/lemma declarations and 24,493 lines across 38 Lean modules, including auxiliary results. A successful build checks formal proofs relative to definitions and explicit hypotheses; it does not discharge an external hypothesis, validate experimental data, or prove that an English paraphrase is faithful.
