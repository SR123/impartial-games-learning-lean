2 Explain this

With all BBB structured heads, the rollout outcomes reconstruct the nim-sum exactly and a shallow readout plays an optimal move. The cost is the rollouts themselves: O(BN2B)O(BN2^B)O(BN2B) simulated moves per decision.

Multi-head rollouts and the outer controller, tick by tick

Exact construction trace Every value is computed from the construction’s definitions for the example shown.

Policy πj\pi_jπj​ subtracts 2j2^j2j from the first heap of size at least 2j2^j2j, and otherwise subtracts 1 from the first nonzero heap. A rollout lets both simulated players follow πj\pi_jπj​ until no move is left; its outcome bit is the parity of the number of moves. Step through the outer controller one tick at a time: it resets, runs head 0's rollout move by move on a working copy of the board, records the outcome when the rollout ends, reloads the original board for the next head, and, in the all-head schedule, finally reads out a move. Every outcome bit here is produced by actually running the rollout; nothing is supplied from outside. The early-exit schedule (Figure 3) stops at the first outcome 1 and only classifies the position.

Readout

σ0 = o0 = 1; σ1 = o1 ⊕ o0 = 0; σ2 = o2 ⊕ o0 = 0, so σ = 1. The readout tests every heap in parallel and takes the first that decreases:

  • heap 0: 3 ⊕ 1 = 2 smaller
  • heap 1: 4 ⊕ 1 = 5 not smaller
  • heap 2: 6 ⊕ 1 = 7 not smaller

Move: set heap 0 to 2 → (2, 4, 6), nim-sum 0.

Counting the work

  • Simulated moves M = 13 + 7 + 7 = 27 (3 heads run).
  • Evaluations M + 2B + 2 = 27 + 6 + 2 = 35: 27 move updates, 3 terminal tests, 3 outcome updates, 1 reset, 1 readout.
  • Bound on M: BN(2B − 1) = 63.
  • Each single evaluation is a shallow circuit: one policy step has Boolean depth ≤ 17 (≤ 36 threshold layers), the readout ≤ 19 (≤ 40), one controller tick ≤ 34 (≤ 70). Those bounds are per evaluation; the number of evaluations grows with the board. That no shallow family computes the complete board-to-move map is a separate statement, conditional on the named classical input that parity is not in AC0.

How this relates to the Lean proof. Each tick is one step of Shootout.ostep, with the policy step SelectCircuit.policyIdx inside the rollout registers RolloutExec.rsStep. Rollout.run_parity identifies the outcome bits with σ0\sigma_0σ0​ and σj⊕σ0\sigma_j\oplus\sigma_0σj​⊕σ0​, and Shootout.ah_counts proves that the all-head schedule takes M+2B+1M+2B+1M+2B+1 controller ticks, the reset included; with the single readout this gives M+2B+2M+2B+2M+2B+2 evaluations. The browser re-implements these steps; the agreement is checked against Lean's own evaluation of them.

What this does not show. Simulated moves and circuit evaluations are different counts: the MMM moves are part of the M+2B+2M+2B+2M+2B+2 evaluations. The depth bounds (17 per policy step, 19 for the readout, 34 per controller tick) apply to one evaluation each. The complete board-to-move computation repeats them a number of times that grows with the board. That no shallow family computes the complete map is a separate statement (Proposition 5.14 and the text after it), conditional on the named classical input that parity is not in AC0\mathrm{AC}^0AC0.

`Wins sel p`: the player to move at `p`, always playing the successor chosen by `sel`, wins against EVERY legal opponent. The selected move is legal, and after every legal opponent reply the player is again in a `Wins` position. Because the predicate is inductive, every such play is finite and ends with the opponent facing a terminal board (normal play: the opponent loses).
