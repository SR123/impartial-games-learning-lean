Proposition 5.5Matching the opponent's nimber difference restores nim-sum zero

Checked theorem Lean checks a formal statement corresponding to this claim.

1 Paper text

Proposition 5.5 (Local restoration principle) Suppose the opponent moves from a position QQQ with nim⁡(Q)=0\operatorname{nim}(Q)=0nim(Q)=0 to a position Q′Q'Q′ (so it is now our turn). Let Δ(Q,Q′)=nim⁡(Q)⊕nim⁡(Q′)\Delta(Q,Q')=\operatorname{nim}(Q)\oplus \operatorname{nim}(Q')Δ(Q,Q′)=nim(Q)⊕nim(Q′) denote the nimber difference caused by the opponent’s move. If we respond with a move Q′→RQ' \to RQ′→R such that Δ(Q′,R)  =  Δ(Q,Q′),\Delta(Q',R) \;=\; \Delta(Q,Q'),Δ(Q′,R)=Δ(Q,Q′), then nim⁡(R)=0\operatorname{nim}(R)=0nim(R)=0.

2 Explain this

The idea. A NIM position with nim-sum zero is "balanced": whoever must move from it loses against correct play. Any move changes one heap, say from xix_ixi​ to xi′x_i'xi′​, and this unbalances the position by exactly δ=xi⊕xi′\delta = x_i \oplus x_i'δ=xi​⊕xi′​. If the reply changes some heap by the same XOR difference δ\deltaδ, the two changes cancel and the balance returns.

A small example. In Figure 2, Q=(3,5,6)Q=(3,5,6)Q=(3,5,6) has nim-sum 3⊕5⊕6=03\oplus5\oplus6=03⊕5⊕6=0. The opponent plays 5→45\to45→4, so δ=5⊕4=1\delta=5\oplus4=1δ=5⊕4=1. The reply 3→23\to23→2 also has 3⊕2=13\oplus2=13⊕2=1, and R=(2,4,6)R=(2,4,6)R=(2,4,6) has nim-sum 2⊕4⊕6=02\oplus4\oplus6=02⊕4⊕6=0 again.

Why it matters. The reply never needs the nim-sum of the whole board. It needs only the two most recent frames: δ\deltaδ is read off the one heap that changed, and a cancelling heap is found by a local bit test. That is why a shallow two-frame network can play this response although no shallow single-frame network can evaluate the nim-sum (Theorem 5.2).

Caveats.

  • The promise matters. If nim⁡(Q)≠0\operatorname{nim}(Q)\neq0nim(Q)=0, matching the difference only reproduces the old value, nim⁡(R)=nim⁡(Q)\operatorname{nim}(R)=\operatorname{nim}(Q)nim(R)=nim(Q) (see the initialisation boundary).
  • Restoration is conditional: it keeps a won position won once a zero-nim-sum position has been established. It does not find the first winning move from an arbitrary position.
  • The previous frame must be the board the opponent moved from, that is, the player's own post-move board.

    Bit k of δ is the OR of column k of the XOR table: 001 = δ = 1.

    At most one heap changed, so at most one row is nonzero and the OR equals the XOR of the rows, which is nim(Q) ⊕ nim(Q′).

How this relates to the Lean proof. The selector is Family.restS (B, N): the local difference circuits LocalRestore.dC, the candidate tests of SelectCircuit.varSel and the priority encoder PriorityEncoder.encode, read on the two-frame input LocalRestore.tf. Family.rest_semantics proves that, on the promised domain, these formulas and the compiled network output a legal move that restores nim-sum 0. Outside the promise the circuit still computes the values shown, and no theorem says that the result restores 0.

What this does not show. The selector does not find a first winning move from an arbitrary board. Given a zero-nim-sum predecessor it maintains the invariant (Theorem 5.12(iii)). The boxes of Figure 7 group operations; the stages here group gates, not network layers.

3 How this is formalised

Objects. A board is a list of natural numbers; nimSum folds XOR over it.

The proposition. Writing q,q′,rq,q',rq,q′,r for the nim-sums of Q,Q′,RQ,Q',RQ,Q′,R, the paper's proof is the identity r=q′⊕Δ(Q′,R)=q′⊕(q⊕q′)=qr = q' \oplus \Delta(Q',R) = q'\oplus (q\oplus q') = qr=q′⊕Δ(Q′,R)=q′⊕(q⊕q′)=q under the hypothesis Δ(Q′,R)=Δ(Q,Q′)\Delta(Q',R)=\Delta(Q,Q')Δ(Q′,R)=Δ(Q,Q′). Lean states it in exactly this form, conditional_restoration: if q=0q=0q=0 and q⊕q′=q′⊕rq\oplus q' = q'\oplus rq⊕q′=q′⊕r then r=0r=0r=0. No legality assumption is needed for this step. one_heap_difference checks the sentence before it: when one heap changes from aaa to bbb, the two nim-sums differ by a⊕ba\oplus ba⊕b.

What else is checked. The shallow selector mentioned right after the proposition, its network form and the whole-game strategy are in Section 5.3.1, paragraph 2. Without the zero-nim-sum promise, matching deltas does not force zero (Rollout.matching_deltas_do_not_force_zero; see Section 5.4, “The initialisation boundary for restoration”).

The proposition is the algebra in conditional_restoration, stated for the three nim-sums q=nim⁡(Q)q=\operatorname{nim}(Q)q=nim(Q), q′=nim⁡(Q′)q'=\operatorname{nim}(Q')q′=nim(Q′), r=nim⁡(R)r=\operatorname{nim}(R)r=nim(R).
