1 Paper text

Finite-state shootout controller (FSM over policy heads). The multi-channel classifier can stop early after a nonzero channel outcome, without changing its Boolean decision rule (the OR of all channel bits). This limited scheduling optimisation does not give the single-channel readout an unrestricted sequential controller. To make this adaptivity explicit (without changing the AC0\AC^0AC0 constraints on each head), it is convenient to view head-selection as a small finite-state controller that sits outside the heads and only sees the terminal win/loss bit of each rollout.

2 Explain this

The multi-channel classifier only needs to know whether some channel bit is 111 (that is, whether σ≠0\sigma\ne0σ=0). So it may stop as soon as one rollout returns 111. This early exit saves rollouts but leaves the decision rule unchanged: it is still the OR of all channel bits.

Caveat: early stopping is a scheduling optimisation for classification. It does not give the single-channel readout extra power, and it is not enough for move selection (see the next paragraphs).

Multi-head rollouts and the outer controller, tick by tick

Policy πj\pi_jπj​ subtracts 2j2^j2j from the first heap of size at least 2j2^j2j, and otherwise subtracts 1 from the first nonzero heap. A rollout lets both simulated players follow πj\pi_jπj​ until no move is left; its outcome bit is the parity of the number of moves. Step through the outer controller one tick at a time: it resets, runs head 0's rollout move by move on a working copy of the board, records the outcome when the rollout ends, reloads the original board for the next head, and, in the all-head schedule, finally reads out a move. Every outcome bit here is produced by actually running the rollout; nothing is supplied from outside. The early-exit schedule (Figure 3) stops at the first outcome 1 and only classifies the position.

Classification

An outcome 1 was observed at head 0, so σ(P) ≠ 0 and the controller stops after 1 of 3 heads. This schedule decides whether the position is winning; it does not collect the bits a move needs.

Counting the work

  • Simulated moves M = 13 (1 head run).
  • Evaluations: 1 reset + M moves + 1 terminal test + 1 outcome update = 16. The early-exit schedule ends with its classification; it reads out no move.
  • Each single evaluation is a shallow circuit: one policy step has Boolean depth ≤ 17 (≤ 36 threshold layers), the readout ≤ 19 (≤ 40), one controller tick ≤ 34 (≤ 70). Those bounds are per evaluation; the number of evaluations grows with the board. That no shallow family computes the complete board-to-move map is a separate statement, conditional on the named classical input that parity is not in AC0.

3 How this is formalised

  • Misc.shootout_spec: started at head 000 with fuel B+1B+1B+1, the depicted controller outputs the OR of the BBB outcome bits, using at most BBB queries, and exactly one query when head 000 reports 111.
  • Misc.channels_or: with the exact structured heads, the OR of the channel bits is "nim⁡(P)≠0\operatorname{nim}(P)\ne0nim(P)=0".
  • Shootout.ee_spec: the bit-level outer controller with the early-exit schedule stops after exactly the predicted number of ticks with decision [nim⁡(P)≠0][\operatorname{nim}(P)\ne0][nim(P)=0], matching the abstract controller.
