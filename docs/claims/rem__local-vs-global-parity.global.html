Remark 2.3 (global parity)Global parity of n = NB bits is not in AC0

1 Paper text

Remark 2.3 (Local vs. global parity) When we say “parity is hard” we mean global parity across the full Boolean input of length n=NBn=NBn=NB. In contrast, parity on O(1)O(1)O(1) bits is trivial in AC0\AC^0AC0 (hardwire the truth table), and more generally XOR of a constant number of BBB-bit blocks is AC0\AC^0AC0-computable. This local/global separation underlies both the two-frame bypass via the nimber difference Δ(P,P′)\Delta(P,P')Δ(P,P′) and the recurrent bypass, which composes local XOR updates over a growing number of steps.

Generated from paper_jmlr.tex, lines 329–334.

2 Explain this

Global parity of all nnn bits cannot be computed by constant-depth, polynomial-size circuits of AND, OR and NOT gates. This is the classical lower bound behind every negative result of the paper.

3 How this is formalised

The classical theorem is not proved in Lean. It enters as the named hypothesis ClassicalBoundary.HastadNegl, the negligible-correlation form of Håstad (2014), Corollary 3.2, in the classical circuit conventions. ClassicalBoundary.parityNotAC0_of_hastad proves that it implies the worst-case family statement Barrier.ParityNotAC0 (no family of fixed-depth, polynomial-size formulas computes parity of mmm bits for all large mmm), including the convention adapters between classical and Lean circuit measures.
