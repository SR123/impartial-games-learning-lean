Section 5.1, paragraph 2Weak mastery = moving to a P-position at every decision history consistent with the agent's own play

Checked theorem Lean checks a formal statement corresponding to this claim.

1 Paper text

We use standard impartial-game terminology: a PPP-position is losing for the player to move, and an NNN-position is winning. For NIM, Theorem 4.4 says that PPP-positions are exactly the positions with nim-sum 000. Equivalently, weak mastery requires the agent to move to a PPP-position at every one of its decision histories consistent with its own earlier actions, starting from an initial position winnable in its designated role. This distinction allows a simple strategy to master a family without solving positions it never encounters under its own play; Appendix D gives examples.

2 Explain this

This paragraph restates weak mastery in game-theoretic terms. In NIM the losing positions for the player to move (PPP-positions) are exactly those with nim-sum zero. A strategy weakly masters a family exactly when, starting from any position its role can win, it moves to a PPP-position at every decision point that its own earlier play can lead to, whatever the opponent does.

That lets a simple strategy master a family without solving positions it never meets. The pairing strategy in Appendix D is the standard example.

3 How this is formalised

  • Mastery.secure_iff_guarantee: winning against every legal opponent is equivalent to the history condition "on every consistent history at the agent's turn, the prescribed action exists and is legal". An absent or illegal action is therefore a loss, never a vacuous success.
  • Mastery.weakMastery_iff_optimal: weak mastery holds iff, from every winnable initial board, at every consistent history at the agent's turn the strategy plays a legal move to nim-sum zero.
  • Mastery.roleWinnable_first: the first player can win from P0P_0P0​ iff its nim-sum is nonzero (the second player iff it is zero).
